# From stated guidance to observable application

The appointment comparisons showed a delivery gap: relevant guidance could be read while rendered type, repeated labels and component scope contradicted it. A model's `guidance-used.json` is therefore a claim, not compliance evidence.

This candidate adds project-owned decision checks to the existing browser adapter. A contract records selected type bounds, internal horizontal text fit, overlapping text fragments, short action-label wrapping and deliberately stationary hover targets. Missing or hidden targets remain unverified; mismatches are retained as failed evidence steps. This is not a style scorer. No universal font, color, radius or type-size limit is introduced.

The adapter now includes a 1000px intermediate viewport. The rejected A date headings passed horizontal fit at 360px and 1280px, but failed at intermediate widths including 1000px. Endpoints and whole-page overflow had missed the local problem. Text range bounds are an approximation, particularly for intentional overlapping typography; inspect the reported fragments. Vertical font-metric overhang is not automatically treated as an overflow defect.

The next development repair separates construction and implementation. A bounded Luna context receives the factual brief, user feedback and relevant local guides. It produces three actual construction renders with recorded type/footprint decisions. A fresh Luna context receives those sources and rendered images before choosing and building. This changes execution as well as instruction delivery; it is not an instructions-only ablation. The brief is familiar and the feedback supplied, so it is not held-out evidence or an unassisted fresh benchmark.

Validation: Python/package checks, browser regression for decision mismatches, and adapter integration proving that mismatch reports survive as failed steps. Functional and visual acceptance remain separate. No promotion or superiority claim follows from passing geometry checks.
