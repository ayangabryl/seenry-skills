# Domain skill evaluation

The package validator proves the skills are installable and their links resolve. It cannot prove an agent selected the right skill, read it, applied it, or produced better design. Keep these as separate observations.

## Route checks

Use fresh contexts with realistic requests, such as: revise a rendered headline without changing the brand; repair a palette whose secondary text disappears on cards; diagnose a 320px form; review a keyboard-only account recovery path; rewrite a claim whose supporting evidence is absent; compare two distinct openings; stress a long-label control; explain a source interaction from a recording. Record the automatically selected skill names and the exact files read. A link in an entrypoint is not evidence that the target file was opened.

## Output checks

For a build, fix the brief, source media, model, effort, time allowance and allowed assets **before** comparing versions. Save the first and final wide/narrow renders, source and output hashes, implementation, interaction checks, model trace and any repair. Review anonymously at ordinary display size. Judge task clarity, original type direction, subject material, working states, accessibility evidence and source fit separately. A type system with acceptable contrast can still be generic; a beautiful capture can still have inert controls.

Run focused craft reviews as well as a holistic review when a specific failure matters. In the first Stillroom trial, a holistic review and an initial focused typography review both accepted a tracked-kicker/italic-phrase/caption combination. After the typography guide was changed to judge the combined signature, a fresh reviewer with the same screenshots and a blind prompt identified it and proposed a concrete subtraction. This supports detection on that case; it does not establish general prevention. Preserve before and after captures if applying the recommendation.

A second fresh build read the revised skills and avoided that exact stack, but still chose a genre-default plant-studio treatment: a category label, large soft serif and colored phrase. An initial focused review accepted it; after the guide explicitly required brand or task evidence beyond a genre mood, a new blind review identified the default and proposed a task-led hierarchy. The builder's first output therefore remains a prevention failure. Do not turn a detection improvement into a consistency claim.

The optional independent gate blocked that second build with `Revise` from both typography and whole-screen critics. After an externally critiqued repair, typography returned `Keep`, while the whole-screen critic still returned `Revise` for reference-like split composition and weak immediate plant comparison. The gate remained blocking. This is a concrete example of why a specialist pass must not be reported as approval of the whole design.

Two further repairs showed why reviewing the rendered task matters. The next whole-screen pass rejected a mobile sequence that recommended a plant before asking about light, a desktop action detached from the selected plant, and an ambiguous low-light cue. After those were fixed, it rejected sunlit photos beside no-direct-sun advice and a selected choice identified only by fill. Typography returned `Keep` in both runs. These are independent static-screen findings, not proof that the remaining interaction works; the builder separately exercised selection and navigation states. Preserve each gate's hashed input captures so a later repair cannot rewrite what was judged.

A fifth phase replaced those two concept photos with lighting-consistent edits and added a quiet persistent check to the selected choice. Both fresh gate reviewers returned `Keep` on the 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844 opening captures. This clears that specific first-slice gate after several externally guided repairs. It does **not** demonstrate reliable first-pass prevention, reference-level quality across briefs, complete-page quality, or accessible behavior; the static reviewers explicitly could not verify interaction, focus, screen readers or lower-page content.

A separate native-app consumer trial used a Seenry-indexed expense flow as task research and built an original SwiftUI group-trip ledger on the iPhone 17 Pro Max simulator. The builder read `seenry-apps` and the relevant craft/review skills, preserved first-pass captures, then exercised add, validation, split calculation, cancellation and persistence. Two fresh reviewers, given only the brief and five first-pass captures, both returned `Revise`. They identified low-contrast entry prompts, an oversized summary that buries records, ambiguous sample-data copy, a completion notice obscuring a balance row, a weak invalid-field state, and no visible correction path after saving. The builder's own final report acknowledged no edit/delete path. This is another prevention failure despite skill reads and functional checks. Treat the findings as concrete app-flow guidance, not evidence that every app needs the same layout or controls.

The app repair added an edit path and clearer field errors, moved the split preview beside its choices, shortened the populated summary, removed fixture-like sample entry and duplicate Add controls, and changed an unrecorded “Settled” balance to “No balance.” Standard Xcode build and simulator add/edit/cancel flows pass on the repaired source. A later review was blocked by screenshots taken during launch and sheet animation, and an edit capture taken before the third participant was selected; recapture after animations and preserve the action order. The [app flow gate](../../skills/seenry-apps/scripts/independent_flow_gate.py) formalizes independent visual and flow review on ordered captures. Its verdict covers the supplied images, not native behavior or general design quality.

On settled captures of the repaired flow, the first app gate returned `Keep` for flow and `Revise` for visual quality. The visual critic still found that Add moved between the empty state, expense list and balance toolbar, and that form examples and section headings remained faint. This was a useful detection despite the builder's multiple repairs. The next repair makes Add persistent and labeled across overview states and gives the form cues explicit darker colors; it needs a fresh rendered review before any cleared claim.

On the next captures, flow again returned `Keep`, while visual review returned `Revise` for an expense row that listed names without explaining they were the sharers and a split preview that said “about” for amounts dividing exactly. The repair labels the relationship and distinguishes exact shares from one-cent rounding. This remains detection and repair evidence; do not infer first-pass prevention from it.

On the final seven ordered captures, fresh visual and flow reviewers both returned `Keep` with no findings. The Xcode build and scripted simulator add, invalid, save, discard and edit paths passed separately. The gate cleared this one pictured flow after multiple review-led repairs; it cannot certify VoiceOver, Dynamic Type, all data boundaries, or consistent first-pass quality on another brief.

A third fresh consumer trial built a fictional, six-clip game sound catalog after reading the Seenry entrypoint and the relevant typography, layout, color and accessibility skills. The clips are original procedural audio. Its first pass played, filtered, searched, downloaded and handled an empty result in browser checks, but both opening-screen critics and a separate full-page critic returned `Revise`. The phone's first Play action began near the bottom of the initial view; repeated introductions, small progress text, a clipped filter and icon-only Download displaced the catalog task. This is another prevention failure even though the builder read the instructions and self-tested behavior.

Review-led repairs removed duplicate framing, exposed each clip's category and named Download action, enlarged time and waveform cues, gave phone filters deliberate tap areas, and made prototype license copy speak only about the offered clips. The phone's first row moved from about 679px to 433px while a 320px overflow check remained clear. Browser checks still passed playback, pause, filter, search, empty/reset, a WAV download and reduced motion. After the final mobile type adjustment, both fresh opening-screen reviewers returned `Keep` on 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844 captures; a separate fresh critic returned `Keep` on the complete desktop and mobile pages. These static judgments do not verify the interactive checks or accessibility beyond what was separately exercised. Do not turn the repaired result into first-pass consistency evidence.

The optional web gate now has a `full-page` scope for the final page. On the preserved final sound-catalog captures, that scope staged the brief and full-page images with SHA-256 hashes and returned `Keep` from a fresh whole-screen reviewer. It still saw only the default pictured state, so the separate browser interaction audit remains necessary. This check closes the workflow gap where the opening gate could pass while a lower-page sentence or section remained unreviewed; it does not improve the builder's first draft by itself.

A fourth fresh consumer trial built a fictional poster-proof comparison tool with an input-controlled reveal, two complete SVG directions, local preference state and a return path. It read six Seenry entrypoints, down from twelve in the preceding connected-screen trial. Its first valid phone render placed full-proof controls below the decision panel and made the drag guidance tiny; both fresh opening reviewers returned `Revise`. The builder repaired the hierarchy, demonstrated intermediate drag and reversal frames, and exercised pointer, keyboard, save/change and 320px reflow. One earlier 390px browser capture was a scaled capture artifact and is preserved separately; it is not counted as a layout defect.

The repaired split view still concealed the headline treatment of both complete posters. A separate fresh art-direction review returned `Revise` for that comparison and the weaker of the two full directions. The optional web gate now accepts up to four named `--state name=image.png` captures. In a trial run with full A, full B and saved B, its fresh whole-screen reviewer returned `Keep` while the typography reviewer returned `Revise` because the default split hid the main type difference. A full-page reviewer also returned `Revise` for a handle clipped at both full-proof endpoints; the private repair kept the handle fully inside the frame at 390px and 320px while retaining the actual reveal bounds. A fresh full-page review of the repaired A, B and saved-B captures returned `Keep`, with input and skill hashes. That verdict does not overturn the earlier first-screen typography finding or the separate art-direction critique. Live reduced-motion and 720px reflow checks passed separately. Static captures cannot prove drag, touch, keyboard or motion timing; the browser checks and frame sequence support those narrower claims. The private prototype and media are not bundled with the skill. This is detection and repair evidence, not consistent first-pass studio-grade output.

Two same-brief replays tested whether changing the early workflow prevents that failure. The first builder implemented and described desk and gallery openings, but both retained the same cropped split; it could not capture its own browser renders. External HTTP captures showed the near-identical phone previews. A fresh typography reviewer returned `Revise` for a repeated oversized title and a familiar atmospheric B proof, while a whole-screen reviewer returned `Keep`. Live browser checks passed drag reversal, keyboard, full views, local save/reload and 320px reflow. The second builder correctly named the split's risk in its decision record, yet its two early studies changed surrounding layout rather than the decisive preview. Two fresh reviewers independently blocked those studies. The [direction study gate](../../skills/seenry/scripts/direction_study_gate.py) formalizes that question-specific review with hashed inputs; it returned `Revise` on the preserved study images. This is a detection gain, not first-pass prevention. Both replays had their first valid captures made externally after the builders' browser access failed, so their builder-only visual decisions remain unverified.

The second builder later revised the poster headlines into both visible halves, but an independent first-screen gate still returned typography `Revise` for a joined mobile heading and the generic serif/glow B proof. A parent repair fixed the heading, built a different B proof from the supplied 20:00 time rather than a glow motif, and moved the phone preference controls into the first view. Fresh review of that repair remained `Revise`: the displayed poster metadata was very small, and the equal split concealed B's defining full-width time field. The repair is parent-assisted, not first-pass model evidence. The report also exposed a review-workspace flaw: a reviewer staged inside the trial searched sibling reports and timed out. Both web gates now stage supplied inputs in temporary reviewer workspaces and copy their logs, structured reports and hashes to the requested output directory. This reduces contamination from earlier feedback; it does not make a model critique authoritative or prove the current design is ready.

A fifth fresh consumer trial built a local kiln schedule for a ceramics studio after reading the shortened Seenry entrypoint, layout and review guides, and two inspected Seenry app captures. Its [brief and captures](artifacts/firing-room-20260929/manifest.json) preserve the decision evidence. Its two early studies genuinely changed the decisive phone hierarchy: an oversized instrument panel delayed the queue; a compact status rail exposed the first piece sooner. A fresh direction gate run outside the builder's sandbox returned `Keep` for the compact study. The first complete wide and phone renders still earned `Revise` from an independent full-page reviewer: unselected phone rows painted empty detail bands. The builder's later browser pass found and removed those bands, plus a wrapping timestamp, duplicated selected-piece copy, broken detail columns and an ambiguous Hold glyph. It exercised selection, Queue/Running, hold/release, reload persistence and keyboard focus; an external Playwright check confirmed those outcomes, no 320px overflow and reduced-motion CSS behavior. A fresh full-page review of correctly sized default, held and running captures returned `Keep`. This is a repaired single-brief result, not first-pass consistency.

The trial exposed two evidence boundaries. The builder's nested CLI reviews could not initialize inside its workspace sandbox; the same staged inputs ran successfully from outside it. Its browser export labeled 320px produced 305px-wide images, leading one reviewer to infer a missing right gutter. External 305, 320 and 390px browser measurements showed equal 16px gutters and no overflow; correctly sized 320px captures cleared that visual finding. A separate parent audit found that desktop row buttons claimed `aria-expanded` for a mobile detail node hidden at that width. The parent removed that disclosure claim while retaining the visible selected state and retested detail access at desktop and phone widths. This semantic repair is parent-assisted and does not establish screen-reader verification. Preserve the builder's first and final captures, input hashes and the corrected capture dimensions when comparing this trial.

A sixth fresh trial built a fictional harbor-forecast comparison. Its [brief, preserved renders and runnable final source](artifacts/northline-20260929/README.md) show materially different early structures and a first complete page that was functional but failed independent visual review. The direction gate found unlabeled mobile return times and selection by color; the builder corrected the return label before its first full render but missed the selected cue and a timeline tick that caused 390/320px document overflow. A first repair fixed those two defects, while another fresh full-page review found that mobile rows still lacked a local “Best window” label and the 320px detail heading compressed its counter and status. A bounded parent repair cleared a subsequent full-page review on desktop, both phone widths and another selected harbor. Browser checks separately confirmed switching, keyboard activation, rapid reversal, a working detail CTA, no final horizontal overflow and reduced-motion scrolling. This is further evidence that the review loop can detect and repair specific failures; the first pass did not meet the quality bar. The product-craft guide now makes local numeric labels, non-color selection, counter/title reflow and chart-edge measurements an explicit pre-expansion check. Prevention from that new wording remains unproven until a fresh consumer run uses it.

A seventh fresh trial used a shortened Seenry entry point and an expanded typography guide to build a fictional footbridge forecast. Its [first-pass artifacts](artifacts/bridge-signal-20260929/README.md) show two materially different studies and a complete page that avoided the rejected editorial opening while putting status, restriction time, advice and forecast together. An external direction review returned `Revise` for joined words on the phone and an inaccurate study-rail proportion. The complete page corrected the rail's proportions before capture but retained the joined label; a fresh full-page reviewer returned `Revise` for that copy defect. The builder's nested review gate could not initialize, so its own direction remained unverified. A separate parent browser audit confirmed local state switching, keyboard activation, the forecast anchor, no 1440/390/320px document overflow and the joined mobile label; motion timing and screen readers remain untested. This shows a better type direction on one brief and another first-pass quality failure. The subsequent skill wording about responsive copy and chart-label meaning is untested prevention, not proof of consistency.

An eighth fresh trial built a three-item running-order component with drag, touch and keyboard reordering after reading Seenry Motion and inspecting source video frames. Its [preserved source, captures and review reports](artifacts/running-order-20260929/README.md) show a first-pass prevention failure: the working component sat below a generic oversized serif introduction, duration and category labels collided at 320px, and the held-state slot cue was faint. The builder could not render in its sandbox, so the parent captured the preserved first source over localhost. Fresh typography and whole-screen reviewers both returned `Revise`. Parent repairs reduced the page shell, separated metadata, constrained the drag to the useful axis and clarified the slot. A second review still returned `Revise` because Reset became icon-only at 320px. After restoring the label, both reviewers returned `Keep` on desktop, phone, 320px, held and settled captures. External browser checks separately confirmed mouse/touch moves, keyboard Home/End, reset, reversal, Escape cancellation, time calculations, no 320px overflow and reduced-motion styling. This is a repaired single component, not first-pass reliability; the subsequent motion-skill wording needs a fresh consumer test.

For a reference target, inspect its relevant pixels or recording and state its coverage limits. The cases in [reference craft cases](../design-craft-cases.md) are prompts for comparison, not design templates or copied media. If an output shares a grid or palette but misses the source's material depth or task rationale, record that gap. A self-review is diagnostic; user acceptance and independent review are distinct.

## Release rule

Publish skill changes after package checks and at least one meaningful consumer trial for the behavior the change claims to improve. A trial may fail; preserve its first render and report the failure. Do not claim consistent improvement until multiple briefs and independent reviews support it. When a gate catches a defect but the builder still produces it, count detection and prevention separately.
