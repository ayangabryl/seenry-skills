# Form & Impression — design brief

## Lock
Audience: a newcomer comparing three introductory letterpress workshops next week. Object: the three sessions and the paper outcome of each. Decision: select one session for local review. No reservation, payment, seat hold or actual availability lookup occurs. All workshop facts, instructors and seat counts are authored fiction. Dates use the user's local current date, 29 September 2026 (Asia/Manila), so “next week” is 5–11 October 2026.

The demo must show date/time, duration, instructor, seats left and what participants make or bring for every session. Selected state must repeat the exact facts and allow return or another choice. Visible demo labeling. No fake booking CTA.

Supplied identity: name only. Avoid a generic editorial stack of tracked kicker, large serif promise, rule and captions. Avoid a schedule that treats paper as decoration, and avoid artwork so dominant that dates disappear.

## Decisive region
The workshop list and selected specimen/detail. Successful state: a newcomer can distinguish all three sessions, select one with pointer or keyboard, review its outcome and understand that no booking happened.

## Studies and decisions
Study A: schedule-first rows with a large specimen of the active workshop beside them. On phone, each row includes a compact print preview. Task path is short and all three dates align. Study B: specimen-first three-column set with details attached to each sheet. Compare at desktop and phone before expansion. Identity 1: dark blue ink on warm paper, with restrained coral for the selection. Identity 2: coral as the dominant field with a condensed heavy typographic treatment on the same Study A composition. These are separate decisions; a hue swap alone is not the mechanism.

## Layout owners
Page gutter: `.shell`; schedule alignment: `.workshop-list` and `.workshop-row`; selected proof: `.proof-panel`. The selected state is an in-page detail panel, not an implied booking flow.

## Decision after rendered studies
The first direction gate returned **Revise** for both comparisons. The schedule-first arrangement was easier to compare, while specimen-first cards made print outcomes more visible. The identity gate preferred the vermilion print-poster treatment but required readable phone word spacing. The revised page keeps schedule-first rows, puts an uncropped original print sheet with each session, and applies the vermilion identity. The three-word headline now names the decision rather than making a broad promise. Revision 1 independent reviews kept the whole screen but requested larger phone availability/instructor labels and removal of duplicated “Bring”; revision 2 applies those changes.

## Final interaction and integrity
Selecting a workshop updates its persistent row cue, specimen, counter and full review facts. On phones it switches to the detail panel and moves focus to the return button. Return restores the three-option list and focus to its first action. No booking, inventory mutation, payment or external destination exists. Seat counts are fixed fictional data, labeled as such on the page.

## Remaining review finding
The final same-layout identity gate returned Revise: the vermilion direction is preferred but its 390px “THREE WAYS INTO” headline was judged too compressed for immediate word recognition. The final first-screen typography critic returned Keep for the delivered view, so independent critics disagree. The two-substantive-revision limit is reached; this is recorded as unresolved, not silently cleared.
