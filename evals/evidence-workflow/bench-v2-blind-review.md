# Blind visual comparison: A and B

Scope: four supplied desktop and phone screenshots only. This assesses the visible initial booking form, not runtime behavior, accessibility implementation, review screens, or confirmation screens.

| Dimension | Preference | Pixel-based reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Task clarity | B | “Book an inspection” states the purpose immediately. Numbered symptom and time sections establish the sequence, and “Review inspection” identifies the next step. A provides clear supporting instructions, but its poetic headline and multiple section titles delay the task. |
| Hierarchy | B | The form is primary and shop explanation secondary. A gives a very large tinted explanation panel comparable weight to the form, and on phone places that explanation ahead of the input. B keeps the phone sequence focused and folds supplementary information beneath it. |
| Typography | B, slight | B has a consistent, restrained scale and clear label hierarchy. A’s serif headline has more distinctive character and its body copy is comfortable to read, but the combination of oversized editorial headline, uppercase eyebrows, and multiple bold subheads feels busier. B’s smallest gray helper/footer text is a weakness. |
| Phone usability | B | B presents the symptom field near the top and the review action within a much shorter page. Its time buttons appear large enough visually, with clear day grouping. A’s first input begins more than 1,000 pixels down the supplied image after a substantial explanatory panel; it requires considerably more scrolling before the actual task. A’s full-width time rows are easier to scan individually. |

## Material flaws

- A: phone content ordering is the major issue. The noninteractive “Choose a time / No slot selected yet” summary appears before the real time controls and before the symptom field. It creates a false starting point and delays completion.
- A: repeated explanations, stacked headings, and prototype-comparison links add visible noise to a simple booking task. The tall desktop explanation card contains substantial unused space.
- B: small muted helper text and demo disclosures are less comfortable to read than the main form content. The screenshots alone cannot establish contrast compliance.
- B: unselected time options look like ordinary buttons rather than explicit radio choices. The initial screenshot does not show how selection is communicated; this is a visual affordance concern, not a claim of broken behavior.
- Both: no supplied image depicts review or demo confirmation, so the full requested flow cannot be judged from these pixels.

## Professional readiness

B is visually professional enough for this small demo: coherent alignment, disciplined spacing, a clear primary action, and a sensible phone composition. It is solid product UI rather than exceptional visual design.

A is a credible styled prototype with a warmer identity and readable form controls. Its desktop presentation is reasonably polished, but the mobile ordering and extra prototype content keep it below the same level of readiness. Correcting those would matter more than adding decorative polish.

Overall preference: B, chiefly because it makes the task easier to find and complete visually, especially on phone.
