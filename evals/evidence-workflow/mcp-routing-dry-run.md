## Scope and initial decisions

I would take the existing-project path: read brand tokens, form implementation and two or three neighbouring screens; render one neighbour. Inventory the existing fields, selection order, validation, submission and recovery before proposing changes. Preserve existing booking semantics and use the app's components and tokens. This is explicitly a redesign, so research and the critic apply; it does not authorize a new brand or invented booking capabilities.

The immediate research gap is how users select and revise a grooming appointment before committing. I would reuse the initial research response, read its queryEvidence, selectionBasis, warnings and coverage, and inspect its supplied pixels. If not already done, read get_library_guide and the exposed schemas before additional calls. I would not issue another paid research_design_brief simply to repeat the initial broad search.

Candidate decisions, conditional on inspecting pixels consistent with the scenario:

- Famous scheduling homepage labelled Checkout: reject as booking/editing evidence. Record the label/pixel conflict and company-profile-only match. A homepage is not a checkout because metadata says so. No identity borrowing is needed in this established app, so I would not retain it merely for fame.
- Spinner: reject for the completed booking form. At most partial evidence for a visible loading presentation if that specific state matters. It establishes neither successful loading nor the eventual form, and does not establish a library-wide absence.
- Native fitness schedule with time/instructor: partial for comparing time and provider in consistently aligned rows. It is not evidence of grooming fields, web controls, selection feedback, editing, or navigation. Retain it only if the existing form actually needs this comparison.
- Grooming appointment detail without editing: partial for appointment-summary grouping, limited to fields and hierarchy actually visible. It cannot prove that summary values are editable or how back/edit behaves. Its domain fit makes it the most useful next investigation, not a complete solution.

## Documentation friction and unnecessary work

1. Existing-project step 4 mandates "one compare_measurements on the references you keep." The revised research section correctly limits it to 2–8 supported captured pages and a real measurement question. In this scenario a mandatory call would be unsupported or wasteful. Existing brand tokens and native stills make the unconditional instruction particularly problematic.
2. The top-level mandatory check/critic gate conflicts with existing-project step 5's review_board, "fix what you see, then stop," and step 4's no critic loop unless redesign. This scenario does say redesign, so I would use the gate, but ordinary repairs face contradictory finish criteria. Clarify the scoped gate explicitly.
3. Existing-project rules prohibit repo scratch files and new DESIGN.md, while the research guide says keep reasoning in DESIGN.md and the general flow demands .seenry/refs/manifest.json plus review/reference-evidence.json. The specific existing-project exception should win, but the docs need an explicit external-path handoff contract so the gate can find evidence without dirtying the repo.
4. Research says a compact complementary set beats fixed counts and cross-category borrowing is conditional. General exploration still requires one of three variants to use an outside-category reference; the final report asks for three reference screens. This reintroduces quotas and potentially needless calls. For this form, the existing-project route avoids the general exploration by default; "unless ... redesign" can nevertheless tempt agents to run the whole brand exploration loop.
5. The inventory requirement of eight to twelve expected items and adding every standard affordance without a backend can conflict with preserving existing behavior and not inventing capabilities. A booking redesign should derive its inventory from actual tasks and current functionality, not a numeric completeness quota.
6. General typography/color/radius bans and palette-generation commands can conflict with preserving existing tokens. Specific existing-project instructions should override them; no get_tokens identity research or palette generation is justified here.
7. Quick mode says apply supported fixes once and deliver; the check step requires an extra verification round after any edits. This may be sensible verification, but whether it consumes a critic round or only validates fixes is unclear.
8. "Best of three, always" image generation conflicts with the one-line quick-mode instruction of one variant per slot. It is irrelevant to this form and should not trigger imagery tools at all.

The strongest part of the revised research guide is its failure-driven routing: it makes me reject misleading rank/labels, keep useful fragments with limits, and turn the missing editing evidence into a specific next investigation rather than compensate with unrelated craft references.
