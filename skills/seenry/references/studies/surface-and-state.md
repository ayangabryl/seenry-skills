# Surface restraint and specific feedback

Use when a functional interface feels heavy or gives every change the same animation. Inventory what each surface communicates: containing context, editable control, selected object, temporary overlay or warning. Remove duplicated treatments only when their meanings remain clear. A border may aid scanning; a shadow may establish an overlay. Neither is automatically outdated.

Compare the same content with different occupied color areas. Let brand expression, content imagery, action emphasis and feedback have distinct jobs. Neutrality does not establish quality, and a colorful identity need not tint every field. Inspect the dense state as carefully as the opening.

Map each transition to its changing object. Selection moves emphasis among stable choices. Progress updates a persistent value. Disclosure reveals detail from its trigger. Confirmation preserves the committed object and states what changed. Avoid animating the whole card when only a value changes.

Countercase: a scene transition may legitimately change the whole composition. Judge continuity against the task, not a rule that every element stays fixed. Check normal-speed playback first, then diagnose timing with slow or frame inspection. Test rapid reversal, keyboard use and reduced motion.
