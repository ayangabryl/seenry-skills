# Website evidence: an assisted development case

Status: historical browser-inspected candidate; no human acceptance. A later refinement of this same visual premise was rejected for falling short of the requested MCP hero quality (see [the later run](jev-100.md)). This is not a benchmark win or proof that a model improved visual quality.

## Problem and candidate

The original Seenry marketing example led into several small interactive specimens. They exposed individual capabilities, but visitors had to follow a smaller link to inspect a complete website. The candidate makes the existing complete Held study prominent, labels its embedded opening honestly, then offers a focused composition control and an installation path. Both examples are authored teaching material; Held is fictional and uses generated imagery.

## What was actually checked

Complete opening and footer; desktop and narrow layout; no horizontal overflow at 390 and 320; composition switching; installation copying; dialog Escape and focus return. Inspection caught joined words from hidden line breaks and undersized specimen copy; both were repaired. The source hero fits its embedded viewport on desktop and mobile. An opening preview still does not substitute for reviewing the entire linked page.

## Transfer carefully

Use substantial website evidence when the visitor needs to judge website capability. For an urgent repair service, availability and contact may deserve priority. For a returning store customer, a browsable catalog can matter more than a brand story. Preserve the decision criterion (visitor task and inspectable evidence), not this candidate's colors, typography, enormous opening or exact section order.

Jev selected a structure from three textual descriptions and selected different priorities for the service/store countercases. It did not see screenshots, generate the page or validate quality. The primary coding agent built and visually reviewed the result. All guidance here works without Jev; additional briefs and independent human review are needed before claiming repeatable improvement.
