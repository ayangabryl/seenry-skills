# Studies by decision

For observed component relationships, see the [Schwaibold component family](component-family.md): eight object-specific observations, transfer questions and countercases from the supplied recording. Its scope and evidence limits are separate from the proposed exercises below.

Select one or two relevant cases; do not load this entire collection into every stage. Evidence types are explicit. These are not twelve human-approved masterpieces or twelve fully implemented reference reconstructions. The two external observations below were inspected on 2026-09-10. Other entries are original teaching exercises with checks to run. Screenshots and source artwork are not licensed for redistribution by this document.

## 1. Otherkind: work as the primary material — observed reference

Source: [Cloudflare project](https://otherkind.design/projects?project=cloudflare). Observed opening: a large warm image of an interface on a laptop, with a much narrower text column. The image carries much more visual area than the explanation. Intent inference: let the application show what the agency can make. Transfer: acquire and compose actual work before polishing a portfolio grid. Countercase: an unfamiliar service with no explanation may remain unclear. Do not reproduce project imagery or repeat its performance claims as verified facts. Only the project opening was inspected; no claim of full interaction review.

## 2. Unseen: an entrance with a recognizable motif — observed reference

Source: [Unseen](https://unseen.co/). Observed entrance: animated eyes on a cream surface, short positioning, entry choices including entry without audio. Inference: a recurring expressive object can create identity before extensive copy. Transfer: derive a motif from the brand and provide a comfortable ordinary path. Countercase: an urgent service should not put essential access behind an entrance. This observation covers the entrance, not the complete project gallery or motion choreography. A saved 17-second capture does not establish a whole-page study.

## 3. Print studio: sheet to application — proposed working exercise

Task: assess whether a visual identity works in use. Composition: one dominant sheet, stable caption, narrow supporting description. Sequence: close crop → sheet edges → quiet hold → next application. Use [worked scores](../../../seenry-motion/references/worked-scores.md) and its runnable score demo. Verify same progress produces the same state forward/reverse; inspect fallback and reading time. Fails when the artwork is an empty mockup or pinning makes people wait without learning anything. A plot of scroll values alone does not prove good timing.

## 4. Photography studio: coherent collection — proposed material exercise

Task: judge the studio's visual capability. Compare three actual candidate images with similar subject scale, light direction and material texture using the asset board. Keep a single hero crop and a supporting pair; vary composition before changing all colors. Choose the image that leaves space for a truthful service statement while preserving the focal subject at mobile width. Fails when beautiful unrelated stock masquerades as commissioned work. Inspect crops in context, source rights and the consistency of the collection, not just the first image.

## 5. Bookshop: let covers remain individual — proposed commerce exercise

Task: find a book, compare editions and understand price. Keep cover proportions truthful, baselines stable and essential edition metadata adjacent. A subtle neutral surface may support colorful covers; an editorial serif may suit an essay but should not crowd compact prices. Sequence: browse → inspect → select edition → confirm availability. Test long titles, sold-out edition, keyboard and missing cover. Fails when forced uniform crops erase cover design or decorative badges compete with title/price. Familiar commerce controls are a positive control for the anti-slop review.

## 6. Monochrome timer: quiet feedback — proposed component exercise

Task: start, pause and adjust a timer without losing place. Anchor digits, separator and unit; reserve width across 9→10. A filled library play/pause icon in a fixed hit area can need no permanent background when legible. Keep keyboard focus visible. Motion follows logical time/state and reverses from the current visual state. Test repeated pause/resume and reduced motion. Fails when rolling digits hide the actual value or status dots imply activity while paused. Monochrome is justified by focus, not a premium color rule.

## 7. Colorful discovery: separate category from action — proposed palette exercise

Task: explore classes by topic. Let cover imagery carry varied color, use category color consistently and reserve a clear action emphasis. Compare occupied color area on the same layout using the color lab. State selection with text/shape in addition to hue. Test color-vision ambiguity and a dense result set. Fails when every topic card shouts as loudly as the primary action, or an identical accent is used for selection, warning and success. Rich color can pass when hierarchy remains clear.

## 8. Dense inventory: precision is the signature — proposed product exercise

Task: find discrepancies and correct a quantity. Align comparable numbers, keep row actions discoverable and preserve scroll position after edits. Column headings, separators and short uppercase identifiers may be useful here. Sequence: search → inspect row → edit → validate → undo. Test 0, negative/invalid values, long identifiers, 200% zoom and failed save. Fails when a decorative card grid prevents comparison. Do not penalize a conventional table merely because it resembles other inventory systems.

## 9. Upload recovery: continuity is visual quality — proposed flow exercise

Task: complete an interrupted upload. Keep the same file row, name and progress anchor across queued, uploading, failed, retry and complete states. A retry control replaces the failed action in place; do not announce success until a result exists. Avoid indefinite spinner-only feedback. Test cancellation during progress and failure after partial completion. Fails when a “premium” transition removes the filename, resets user choices or hides the recovery path. Use an explicit local simulation in a prototype; never imply a real upload backend.

## 10. Editorial essay: typography earns its place — proposed type exercise

Task: read a nuanced argument. Compare actual introductory paragraph, headings, notes and punctuation across shortlisted families. Keep measure and hierarchy comfortable; use italics for their semantic role rather than making every highlighted word a flourish. Verify glyph coverage and fallback. Fails when magazine styling is applied to dense numeric controls or huge headings repeatedly interrupt reading. Explicitly editorial work is a positive control: a serif/italic treatment must be capable of passing contextual review.

## 11. Mobile studio navigation: fewer competing actions — proposed navigation exercise

Task: understand navigation and reach contact. Measure the real brand width, menu trigger and longest CTA at 320–430px. A single clear menu plus a concise contact action can work; an icon-only external arrow and an adjacent menu must not appear interchangeable. Match visual baselines and retain generous invisible hit areas. Test menu focus, Escape, return focus and overlay scrollbar behavior. Fails when desktop labels are squeezed together or a reserved scrollbar rail wastes mobile width. No specific menu arrangement is universally best.

## 12. Pricing: explain a decision — proposed decision-support exercise

Task: determine an appropriate plan and total commitment. Put comparable terms on common baselines; keep billing period, limits and important exclusions visible. A recommended tier needs a real audience/use reason. Test annual/monthly change, unavailable plan and no-JS truth. Fails when a highlighted tier is invented to create visual hierarchy, an animated price briefly shows the wrong amount or all three plans repeat generic benefits. Minimalism must not hide decision-critical terms.

For a genuine full-page reference study, record top/middle/end at normal viewport, one transition, mobile and missing coverage. These studies demonstrate transfer questions, not curator ratings. Add new inspected evidence to a selected case when it changes a decision; do not collect screenshots solely to increase the count.
