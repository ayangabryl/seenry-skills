# Transfer craft from a visual reference

Use this for a new design or adaptation when the user names an inspiration or asks for comparable visual quality. A requested faithful reproduction uses [reference reconstruction](replication.md) instead. Inspect source pixels at readable size; a listing, caption, score or memory is insufficient evidence. Static images do not establish motion.

## Choose evidence that can answer the brief

Separate a **craft target** (the finish to compare against), a **pattern source** (a useful layout or interaction), and a **content source** (material to display). One artifact can serve more than one role, but a useful pattern is not automatically a sufficient craft target. A famous product with an irrelevant or incomplete capture cannot certify this output. Inspect the particular region or sequence that supports the claimed relationship.

When the brief asks for exceptional visual quality, identify the dominant material carrying the reference: authored imagery, a resolved product surface, typography, narrative reveal, or another visible relationship. Compare its richness and scale with what is actually available for the new work. If the direction depends on an impressive example, build or source that example before arranging a full page around it. A generic dashboard, token palette, or abstract shape study may explain a mechanism without satisfying the intended visual promise. Those materials remain valid when they are themselves the subject or a deliberate fit for the brief.

Do not lower the target to the most convenient search result. If relevant reference pixels cannot be obtained, use supplied/owned evidence or state that the target remains unverified. Avoid filling the gap with confident style language. Research is sufficient when it supports a consequential design choice; collecting more recognizable brands is not the objective.

For a working product surface, add [product craft](product-craft.md): separate the interface from its marketing frame and carry its task hierarchy into connected states.

## Identify what makes the reference work

At the relevant desktop and narrow widths, record the source artifact and the few relationships that dominate the first impression: subject material and how much of the viewport it occupies, crop and layering, type silhouette and scale, balance between dense and quiet regions, surface detail, and the handoff to the next section. Name the purpose of each relationship in that design. Avoid a generic inventory of colors and effects.

Choose which relationships serve the new brief. Translate their jobs with original subject-specific assets, content and identity. A composition driven by two richly worked materials may become noticeably weaker if one is replaced by an empty fill, even when the split and heading positions match. A second original crop, process photograph or generated paper treatment may carry that role; inspect its detail at the final scale. A subtle gradient or line pattern is not automatically a substitute for rich material variation. If a needed image or material is missing, obtain or make an authorized original asset, change the composition, or keep the quality claim unresolved. Do not copy the source artwork, branding, prose or premium recipes into the new product.

## Compare the actual result

Before expanding a full page, render its decisive slice and place it beside the relevant source region at comparable readable widths. Actually load and view both images in an image-capable tool; a saved screenshot path or note describing the intended layout is not a comparison. Record the largest visible gaps in **subject fit, composition, material, type hierarchy and next-section rhythm**; explain their consequence for this brief. Repair the most important gap while direction changes are still cheap. Check whether prominent type crosses or clips at the intended surface boundary. A neat grid, working CTA or analogous headline placement does not by itself establish the source's level of craft.

After implementation, inspect both ordinary and narrow output captures against the corresponding source captures. Preserve source and output paths or IDs, note any missing source state or font, and state the surviving gaps. Judge motion separately from screenshots, using recordings or local playback when it matters. If the comparison cannot be performed, label reference-level quality unverified; do not convert implementation checks or a design rationale into visual parity.

## Resolve the decisive gap before multiplying the design

Use the decisive slice to distinguish three possible failures:

- **Wrong argument:** the composition cannot show why this product or work matters. Reopen what is shown and its reading sequence; do not just change its skin.
- **Weak material:** the composition is sound, but its central image, illustration or product example lacks the specificity or finish needed at that scale. Improve or replace that material before adding motion, gradients or extra sections around it.
- **Unresolved execution:** the idea and material work, but type, crop, alignment, surface relationships or state transitions undermine them. Make the local repair and compare again.

Keep the original reference target through this decision. Describe the largest visible gap and the next revision in ordinary terms; do not average it away with accessibility or runtime passes. If the requested quality is still unmet, deliver a clearly labeled review iteration with the remaining gap. A second screen inheriting a weak system consistently is evidence of reuse, not evidence of strong art direction.

A useful counterexample is a conventional account form inside an established product: familiarity and faithful system reuse may fully satisfy its brief. Do not force hero artwork, novel navigation or a new identity into that task merely to demonstrate creativity.
