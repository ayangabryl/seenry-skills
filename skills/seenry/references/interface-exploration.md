# Find the interface idea before its appearance

Use for a new interface, an expressive opening, or a rejected creative direction. Skip this exploration for a bounded repair or faithful reconstruction. Keep existing brand and accessibility requirements. This is a way to develop and compare ideas, not a recipe for animated lettering.

## Turn the promise into something observable
Write a short chain before choosing layout:

**User need → useful material → meaningful operation → visible consequence.**

Ask: what could someone understand by trying the interface that a sentence or decorative image cannot establish? Use the product's actual material: a route, a document, a collection, a sound, a value or a design. If no useful operation exists, prefer a strong static composition. Do not invent interaction merely to make the screen look creative.

For each candidate, finish: “When the visitor ___, they see ___, which helps them understand ___.” Reject vague endings such as “it feels premium.” Identify the normal action that must remain obvious throughout.

## Generate different mechanisms, not different skins
With the same facts and brand, explore three different organizing ideas. Change how the visitor understands or operates on the material: comparison, rearrangement, direct manipulation, progressive disclosure or an authored static sequence are possibilities, not a mandatory menu.

For each, record:
- The main object and why it belongs to this product.
- What the visitor does, what changes, and what remains anchored.
- What it proves honestly, and what it cannot prove.
- The first readable state, narrow-screen behavior and non-motion alternative.
- Its strongest reason to fail: ambiguity, effort, irrelevant spectacle, missing assets or technical fragility.

Avoid starting from a favorite effect or library. A fluid transition is useful after there is a relationship to explain. One convincing operation is stronger than five unrelated effects competing for attention.

## Extract a principle, then change its application
For each inspected reference write: observed relationship → purpose there → relevant principle → original application here. Separate the source's composition from unobserved motion. Combine compatible principles, not recognizable fragments of several pages. Do not copy another identity, unsupported marketing claim or visitor-facing control labels merely because the reference worked.

Render the decisive state of each idea with real content. Compare them at the same viewport and information budget. For the selected interaction, prototype its initial, changed and returning states before building the rest of the page. Test whether someone can identify the offering and next action before touching anything.

## Worked example: the Seenry skills opening
Need: understand that these are design skills for a coding agent. Material: the same six letters. Operation: choose Type, Layout or Motion. Consequence: see typographic character, arrangement and movement change while the offering and install action remain steady.

The first split headline/sculpture direction demonstrated an attractive asset but said little about the skill. Inspected Dipsy lettering suggested making identity the principal material; Spline's captured creative scene suggested giving the demonstration more space. The resulting live text composition used Seenry's own neutral palette, typography and controls. These static captures informed composition, not measured animation.

The user liked the resulting hero. That is evidence of fit for this project, not proof that a skill autonomously produces this quality or that every hero should use big letters. The transferable move is **make a relevant capability visible through the interface's behavior**.

Transfer examples to explore, not finished prescriptions:
- A route planner could let a visitor change one constraint and see the route respond; the route and itinerary remain the evidence.
- A document tool could expose the same passage in source and revised states, with differences grounded in an actual edit rather than fabricated results.
- An urgent service may benefit most from a clear availability state and immediate contact action. A playground could obstruct its purpose.

## Selection and stopping rule
Choose on relevance, immediate clarity, visual coherence and interaction payoff—not novelty alone. Explain why the chosen mechanism fits better than the rejected ones. Inspect the selected result at desktop and narrow widths, then exercise keyboard, interruption, reversal and reduced motion. Use the layout-verification guide for the actual painted surfaces. Preserve the accepted idea when refining small defects; reopen exploration only when the organizing idea itself fails.

Save the chain, three candidate mechanisms, rendered study paths and selection reason in the project's DESIGN.md. Keep this reasoning out of the visitor-facing interface. Report authored demonstrations as demonstrations; never imply they are independent model benchmarks.
