# Compare dominant type and low-contrast material

Use when a replica's overall layout looks plausible but its primary text or soft background material carries the source's visual character.

Compare source and render at equal physical size. A headline can keep the right words and line break yet have substantially narrower ink. Measure its actual visible bounds and line depth before changing nearby spacing. If the font is unavailable, record the substitution and compare the rendered specimen rather than assuming a CSS size matches.

Quiet material needs its own check: inspect broad light and dark regions around the subject, including gradients or glow behind a product surface. A border-aligned shell can still feel flat when the field supporting it differs. Compare representative patches away from text and edges; a bright-ink threshold cannot measure this material. Repair the largest visible mismatch before small icons.

If a browser runner cannot capture the output, try one other available authorized route. Preserve the failure and leave visual parity unverified when no rendered comparison is possible. Do not repeat the same blocked launch as evidence of fidelity.
