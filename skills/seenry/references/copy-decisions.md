# Write from the offer and the reader's task

Use when creating or revising website copy, headlines, actions or interface messages. This is a decision aid, not a phrase generator. Reuse the project's product facts and voice guidance; do not start a separate marketing document for a small edit.

## Establish the material

Identify the reader, the task bringing them here, what is actually available, the next action, and the evidence for any claimed advantage. Use customer language only when supplied or inspected; invented customer quotations are not research. Mark unknown price, eligibility, counts, timing and guarantees as unresolved rather than filling them with persuasive guesses.

Select the page's argument from those facts. A reference library may need browseable examples before a sales pitch. A checkout needs the charge and renewal terms before reassurance. A showcase should let people use the work. Do not force each into problem → benefits → social proof → FAQ.

## Draft without the layout scaffolding

Write the direct version first: what is it, what can the reader do, and what happens next? Add personality only where it makes that meaning sharper. Avoid automatically generating a heading, a subtitle and helper text for the same thought. An ordinary label is often enough.

Examples for Seenry, using product facts rather than invented results:
- “Elevate your creative workflow” → “Find website screens and interaction references.”
- “Unlock limitless inspiration” → “Browse the reference library.”
- “Design, reimagined” → “Use a reference in your next build.”
- A play control can be icon-only with an accessible name; it does not need a slogan.

These are editing examples, not a universal vocabulary. Do not replace every occurrence of a word mechanically. Technical terms, humour and expressive language can be appropriate to a known audience. Never claim copy was human-written simply because it avoids familiar AI phrases.

## Edit in context

Check meaning, factual support, repetition, voice and action expectation separately. Read the entire flow, including empty/error/success states. Keep the same noun for the same object. Ask whether each sentence changes what the visitor understands or does; remove unsupported adjectives rather than replacing them with synonyms.

Use concrete examples when numbers are unavailable. Do not turn “faster” into “saves four hours” without evidence. Do not add urgency, a guarantee, a free trial or “no credit card” unless the product actually supports it. Conversion claims require an actual experiment; a simulated expert score is not one.

Render the final words at wide and narrow widths. Keep factual meaning when reducing line length. Verify the action does what its label says, and errors explain an available recovery. Keep editorial rationale in the design record, outside customer-facing UI.
