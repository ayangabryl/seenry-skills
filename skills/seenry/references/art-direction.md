# Decide what deserves attention

Use during discovery and planning of a new identity, website or substantial redesign. Make the offering clear before naming a style. The tools below are decision aids, not another visitor-facing section template.

## Establish an argument

Write one plain sentence for each: who this is for; what is offered; what visible evidence supports it; what the visitor can do next. Separate supplied facts from your proposals. If the brief is “design studio”, a coding stack is not evidence that the studio sells technical consulting. Ask about an unknown only when it changes the offering; otherwise use an explicitly fictional, revisable direction.

List the visitor's questions in the order they need answers. Map each section to a different question and to its evidence. Delete a section that repeats an answer without adding proof. A studio can lead with work, an unfamiliar product may need demonstration, and an emergency service should lead with availability and action. No fixed hero → three cards → manifesto → pricing sequence.

## Develop three different arguments with the same facts

Change the organising idea, not only colors, fonts or corner shapes. For example, a sound studio could demonstrate the difference before explaining it, organise work by listening situations, or expose a spatial mixing process. All must still state the service and offer a normal path without sound. These are possible directions, not a mandatory audio interaction.

For each alternative record: dominant material, first reading sequence, a subject-specific operation, what remains still, what it costs the visitor, and the reason it could fail. Make a small composition with candidate media while wireframing. If the direction depends on a hero photograph, prove that a suitable photograph exists before selecting it. Keep the earlier wireframe artifact; a finished layout with a grid overlay is anatomy, not history.

At the surface stage, carry forward content priority, alignment and behavior; reconsider the marks used to explain the wireframe. A heavy region outline, placeholder labelled DEMO, section ordinal or debugging badge is not automatically part of the visual identity. Account for each retained enclosure by its actual grouping or interaction role. Remove study-only labels and empty media blocks before final material review. A wireframe is a structural hypothesis, not a skin to inherit unchanged.

Reject an alternative if swapping its name and noun lets it sell an unrelated business equally well. This is a question for review, not a ban on useful shared controls. Reject three directions that all inherit the same unverified positioning. Reset that assumption before producing three more skins.

## Author a coherent identity

An identity is a relationship between wordmark, type voice, palette, image treatment, graphic behavior and language. Choose two or three relationships the project can carry consistently: e.g. close crops and open margins, compact labels and generous imagery, or a precise material reveal. Use the existing brand when supplied. Do not force a custom logo or rebrand into a narrow task.

Test the chosen relationship in the opening, one dense section, a small control and the footer. It should survive with the display heading removed. A signature may be quiet: an unusual image sequence or a distinctive way to compare work can be stronger than a shader. Demonstrate WebGL/GSAP/Lenis combinations only when they serve a real spatial or narrative idea and preserve the ordinary path.

## Choose surfaces with the actual material

Render shortlists using real copy and actual crops. Judge the type's silhouette, line breaks, numeral width and label legibility; compare at perceived size, not equal CSS size alone. Start with a small set of text roles and expand only for a distinct hierarchy need. Check supported glyphs and fallback before selecting an unusual family.

A light neutral system can make material color dominant. A dark system can support luminous content or prolonged dim use. Neither is inherently premium. Record surface color, occupied area, competing emphasis and image light together. Compare one restrained and one more expressive palette on the same composition when color is uncertain. Do not force three appearances if the product does not need them.

Choose containment from grouping and operation: open spacing for related reading, an enclosure for a distinct manipulable object, stronger fill for an important action. For nested corners, start with the outer radius minus inset for concentric shapes, then inspect small-radius and unequal-padding exceptions. Match control height, label baseline, icon weight and click area; invisible hit padding can preserve a quiet icon action without shrinking its target.

## Plan the whole page and motion together

Storyboard opening → evidence → explanation → decision → closure, changing that order to suit the task. Mark each section's visual load, reading duration and transition. Give dense evidence a quieter neighbor. Avoid making every section shout with the same giant heading or animated entrance.

Choose one leading motion idea where useful, plus restrained task feedback. Write the motion score before implementation using seenry-motion. Specify a static equivalent, reversal and interruption. The opening must remain understandable while assets load; never require a cinematic introduction to reach the service or navigation.

## Research basis and limits

[Design Council](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/framework-for-innovation/) supports understanding the problem and iterating through alternatives. [Dow and colleagues](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/2010/parallel-prototyping/ParallelPrototyping2010-final.pdf) studied human parallel prototyping; this motivates comparison, but does not establish a three-prototype advantage for Flash. These proposed exercises need outcome testing. They are not findings about universal taste.
