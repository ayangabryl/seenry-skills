# Seenry Writing

Read the product's existing words, terminology and brand voice first. Identify who is reading, what they need to decide, what the product truly offers and what action actually works. Separate supplied facts, observed customer language, proposed fiction and unknowns. Do not invent reviews, outcomes, prices, urgency or operational promises.

## Make the page's argument

Write a direct version before adding personality: what is this, why does it matter here, what proof can the visitor inspect and what happens after the action? For marketing, put questions in the order the visitor needs answered. Give each section a new answer or evidence. A slogan, eyebrow and helper sentence that repeat one idea are one idea spread across three layers.

For product UI, keep one noun for one object across labels, menus, confirmation and errors. Match an action label to its actual result. Errors name the problem and available recovery without blame or false reassurance. Success language follows a successful operation, not a click. Write for the input methods the interface supports and for translation where required.

## Edit on the screen

Render the real words at wide and narrow widths. Check line breaks, truncation, label proximity and whether copy pushes the subject or action out of the opening. Read the full path including empty, loading and error states. Compare a shorter version against the original with the same facts; keep distinctive language when it clarifies the offer or belongs to an established voice. Avoid automatic bans on humor or expressive phrasing, but ask whether it makes the decision easier.

Record each unsupported or ambiguous claim and its source or resolution. Conversion improvement requires actual measurement; a reference screenshot cannot prove it.
